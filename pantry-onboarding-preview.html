Why this lands

The honest answer to “how would you know the dates?” — mostly you don’t at first, so the design says so instead of faking it.

Effortless by default

She just gets everything in. No date field at the one moment it’d be most annoying — a 40-item dump. Items land as “on hand · estimated,” and freshness alerts stay silent until a real signal arrives.

Precision is a delegation

“Ask my helper” sends a list of only the ~8 spoilers — where a real date saves a jar. Not a 40-item marathon. The helper reads labels where they exist, eyeballs the herbs where no date does.

No false precision

Sealed goods show the scanned best-before; decanted Kilner jars stay honest estimates. We never claim “100%” — the ceiling is “exact where the label still shows it.”

It self-corrects

Every restock upgrades an estimate to a known date — so the pantry sharpens over a few weeks with zero effort. Confidence is visible and climbing.

The alert earns its keep

Only known-date spoilers trigger a warning — and it’s framed as a recipe to cook tonight, not guilt. Freshness becomes a reason to cook, and quietly saves the money.
