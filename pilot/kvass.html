Method · step 1 of 5

Dice the beets — skin on

Cut 3 medium beets into small cubes. No peeling — the skin helps the ferment. Fill the bottom ⅓ of the jar.

Done looks like: jar one-third full of red cubes.

Screen stays on while you work

Method · step 2 of 5

Add the salt

Spoon in 1½ level tbsp non-iodised salt over the beets. Add a few slices of ginger if you have it.

Sea salt or rock salt — not table/iodised salt.

Done looks like: salt sitting on the beets.

Screen stays on while you work

Method · step 3 of 5

Fill with water — cover the beets

Pour in filtered water to about 2–3 cm below the rim. Every beet must stay under the water.

Filtered water only — tap chlorine can stall it.

Done looks like: beets fully submerged, water near the top.

Screen stays on while you work

Method · step 4 of 5

Lid on — leave it on the counter

Close the lid loosely (or a cloth over the top). Leave it at room temperature, out of the sun.

Done looks like: jar sitting quietly on the counter.

Screen stays on while you work

Method · step 5 of 5

Wait about 5 days — taste daily

Leave it to ferment. Taste a little each day from day 5. Ready when it’s tangy & earthy, then strain and keep it in the fridge.

The rules that keep it safe

Earthy & sour smell = good, that’s what you want.

Any fuzzy mould = tip it out and start again. Don’t taste it.

Keep the beets under the water the whole time.
